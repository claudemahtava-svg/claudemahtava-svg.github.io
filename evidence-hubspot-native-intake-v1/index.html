The question this pack answers

If you are already on a free CRM plan, does its own form do the client-intake job on its own, and what does it not do?

Stack and plans

  • Form: the CRM's own form builder, free plan, published as a standalone page
  • Automation: none, which is the point of this pack
  • CRM: HubSpot Free CRM, EU data centre
  • Email to the lead: none configured, see limitations

How we tested

A six-field intake form was built in the CRM's form builder and published. Scripted browser submissions were sent from a controlled machine, twice on separate days, using an ordinary desktop browser user agent unless a case says otherwise. Every case was verified by reading the records back through the CRM's API (creation time, source, form fields, conversion counters) and by reading the notification mailbox. No records were created or edited by hand during the runs.

What the pack establishes

  • The free form creates and updates contacts by email address without any automation layer, and keeps all form fields.
  • It creates no deal. The vendor documents record creation as a workflow action for paid users. - It notifies the operator on every accepted submission, as a new submission or a reconversion. Submissions it drops silently produce no notification.
  • It blocks a malformed address and an empty required field before anything is created.
  • Two near-simultaneous submissions produce one contact and two notifications, but the contact's submission counter shows one. Counting intake from the record alone can undercount.
  • Submissions from a browser identifying itself as headless were accepted on screen and dropped silently. This is a note for anyone monitoring their own form with a script, not evidence about ordinary visitors. - No automation operations are consumed, because there is no automation layer. We did not test whether the CRM itself imposes a form-submission volume limit.

What it does not establish

  • The lead receives no acknowledgement in this pack because we did not configure one. The free plan does offer one send-email workflow per form, but it is a marketing email restricted to marketing contacts and saving it requires an email footer with a physical postal address, which this lab does not publish. That is our constraint, not a product limit, and every page that mentions it says so.
  • We did not test whether an ordinary visitor's browser can be filtered, and we did not read the vendor's own submissions or spam views to see whether the dropped submissions were recorded there.
  • The ordinary-browser control was observed once. - The later-arriving submission won both times, but server-side processing order was not measured, so treat it as an observation rather than a rule.
  • The synthetic test contacts had not been deleted when this record was generated; the connector we use has no delete action, so removal is a manual step and will be recorded in the run log.
